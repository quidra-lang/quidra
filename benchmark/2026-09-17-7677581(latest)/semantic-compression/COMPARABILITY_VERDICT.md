# Semantic Compression: comparability verdict (AUTHORITATIVE CAVEAT)

After TWO rounds of adversarial audit and one full correction pass, four independent spotcheck
agents examined the contested metrics across all ten languages. **All four concluded the metric
is not yet cross-language comparable, and that residual bias still favours Quidra.**

| Metric | Comparable across languages | Quidra still favoured | Residual blockers |

| Semantic Density (locally recoverable facts) | **NO** | **YES** | 3 |
| Semantic Determinacy | **NO** | **YES** | 1 |
| Semantic Locality | **NO** | **YES** | 7 |

Quidra is no longer an outlier on this metric: on the primary common basis (33 probes) it is SECOND, behind Zig (0.8982 vs 0.9675, scores 100 vs 93.0), and it is charged MORE than Python on three probes (F10.P1 5 vs 3, F06.P2 3 vs 2, F09.P1 6 vs 5). Q1 — same rule, same strictness? Not quite. One split is real and material. At F11.P1, the widest-spread probe in the corpus (B = 2..10), Go, Rust, Zig, C++ and Swift collapse the user-redefinable-subscript family to a single outcome under Rule 1.4.5, while Python, TypeScript, Java and Kotlin enumerate its leaves. Working the three widest-spread probes from the frozen rule and the sources: (a) F11.P1 — Quidra 2 is CORRECT, verified independently against the Quidra spec, not taken on the annotator's word: lossless-only implicit conversion (language.md L419) closes the float/text/class classes against the written `int` destination while admitting uint8/16/32; no operator overloading; no preprocessor.
